01  Overview

Funding that finds the business,
not the other way around.

Small wellness businesses have a hard time borrowing. Banks want an application, a credit check and collateral, and a yoga studio or a salon rarely has the time or the paperwork.

Mindbody already processes those businesses' card sales, so a lending partner can pre-approve an offer from that history. Capital puts the offer inside Mindbody: accept it in a few clicks, get the money in two business days, and repay it automatically as a fixed share of daily sales. My job was to make a financial product that owners never asked for feel simple and trustworthy, and to make it work for owners with many locations.

02  The problem

A loan nobody applied for
has to earn trust fast.

Pre-approved money sounds like spam. Owners are busy, wary of debt and unfamiliar with financing that is repaid from sales instead of a monthly bill. Words like capital fee, repayment rate and estimated payoff mean nothing until you can see them play out on your own numbers.

Capital started in the classic Mindbody software as a single offer on one page. That did not scale. Offers are generated for each location from that location's own sales, so an owner with ten studios has ten offers, ten balances and ten payoff dates. And the platform was moving to a new navigation, so Capital needed a real home in it.

03  My role and how I worked

Designing the whole loop,
from offer to payoff.

I designed Capital end to end on the Payments team: how an owner discovers an offer, chooses an amount, accepts it, and manages the advance afterward. I worked with our product manager and engineers, and inside the rules of the lending partner that underwrites each offer.

I started in grey boxes on purpose. With money involved, the hard part is agreeing on what each page must answer before anyone argues about color, so the low fidelity screens were how product, engineering and the partner signed off on structure. Two decisions shaped the result.

Decision 01

Show the repayment, not just the amount

A big number gets attention, but it does not answer the real question: what will this cost me on a slow week? An owner picks an amount on a slider and the fee and total update as it moves. Below it, a five day chart shows 15% of sample daily sales going to repayment, including a closed day at $0. Pay less on slow days stops being a promise and becomes something you can see.

Decision 02

Design for the owner with ten locations

One offer page works for a single studio and breaks for a chain. I made every location its own offer card, then built a dashboard that adds them up ("You're approved for $72,000"), a table of accepted advances with balance, rate and payoff date, and a detail page for each advance with its full transaction history.

08  Impact

A financial product
owners could read.

Shipped in the platform

Capital moved from a single page in the classic software into the new navigation, reachable from the home feed, the dashboard, Reports and Add-on Services.

One pattern for every location

Offers, balances and payoffs work the same for a single studio and a chain, because every location is its own card and the dashboard does the adding up.

Plain answers in place

Eleven questions owners actually ask, from credit score impact to late fees and prepaying, are answered on the page instead of in a support ticket.

Built to come back

Each advance surfaces the next offer when it is ready ("Your next offer is for $15,000"), turning a one time loan into an ongoing relationship with Mindbody Payments.
